Measured Relaxation Rates

{{ spectral_density_data.rates_svg|safe }}

The three measurements the mapping consumes, on a shared residue axis so a feature in a spectral density can be traced back to the rate that produced it. A negative hetNOE is physically valid and marks flexible regions; it is mapped rather than filtered, though J(0.87ωH) then carries very large relative error.

R2/R1

{{ spectral_density_data.r2_over_r1_svg|safe }}

The classic route to an overall correlation time without the NOE, but not exchange-free: Rex inflates R2 and so inflates the ratio. An elevated point is ambiguous between slow tumbling, diffusion anisotropy and chemical exchange, which is why the R1·R2 product follows.

R1·R2

{{ spectral_density_data.r1r2_svg|safe }}

The product is far less sensitive to diffusion anisotropy than R2/R1, because the anisotropy dependences of R1 and R2 largely cancel in it. A residue standing above the trimmed mean therefore points at chemical exchange rather than at an orientation effect (Kneller, Lu & Bracken, JACS 2002). The trimmed mean and ±2σ band are an orientation aid, not a hypothesis test — the multi-field χ² is the test this module offers.
